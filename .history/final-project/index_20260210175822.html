A Complete Guide to Maggot Farming: Training, Production, and Profitability Introduction Maggot farming, also known as maggot production or larvae farming, is the practice of breeding fly larvae—mainly from the Black Soldier Fly (BSF) or housefly—for commercial use. Maggots are widely used as: Fish feed (catfish, tilapia, etc.) Poultry feed Animal protein supplement Organic waste recyclers Bait for fishing Because of their fast growth, low cost, and high protein content, maggot farming is becoming one of the most profitable small-scale agribusinesses. Benefits of Maggot Farming Low Startup Cost – Can begin with simple materials Fast Production Cycle – Harvest in 7–14 days High Demand – Used in fish and poultry farming Eco-Friendly – Converts waste into valuable protein High Profit Margin – Minimal running expenses Types of Maggots Commonly Farmed 1. Housefly Maggots Fast breeding Easy to manage Suitable for beginners 2. Black Soldier Fly Larvae (BSF) Higher protein Less smell Longer lifespan More profitable commercially For beginners, housefly maggots are recommended. Step 2: Prepare the Rearing Box Make small holes for drainage Place wire mesh on top Add prepared feed inside Step 3: Attract Flies Place boxes outdoors in shaded areas Expose feed for 6–12 hours Flies will lay eggs Cover after eggs appear. Step 4: Hatching and Growth Eggs hatch in 24–48 hours Larvae feed actively Spray lightly with water daily Avoid flooding Step 5: Harvesting Harvest after 5–10 days when maggots are thick and active.
